Research Interests & Background

What I'm looking for, and how I got here

Research interests

I'm seeking graduate research opportunities at the intersection of Neural Engineering, Computational Neuroscience, and AI in Neurology — specifically, building systems that combine sensor-level signal acquisition with machine learning to detect and understand neurological conditions earlier. My work so far sits exactly at that intersection: hardware that captures physiological signal, and models that interpret it. I'm open to a research group whose focus leans toward any one of these three, as long as that core combination — signal acquisition plus ML-based interpretation — is central to the work.

How I got here

My path runs from hardware to modeling to neurology. I started by building biomedical devices — an IV drip controller, a dry bath incubator, a blood pressure monitor — which taught me how physiological signals behave at the sensor level. That led me to ask what those signals could reveal computationally, which is what pulled me into machine learning: my Alzheimer's research applies that same signal-level thinking to speech and cognitive data instead of voltage readings. Neural engineering is the natural next step — it's where hands-on signal acquisition and ML-based interpretation meet the nervous system directly.

Background

I'm completing a Bachelor of Science in Biomedical Engineering Technology at Foundation University School of Science and Technology (expected graduation 2027), with coursework spanning medical device development, digital logic design, and AI-based healthcare research.

Python, MATLAB, Arduino / embedded systems, and machine learning.
